# Campaign Arc Plan

## Campaign shape

The campaign has two layers:

- **Loop Zero** teaches the business, expedition, social, political, and dungeon-pressure systems while presenting a plausible story about saving a tavern.
- **The Remembered Campaign** begins after the first destruction of the Hearth. Repeated loops turn previously ordinary scenes into strategic choices and expose the real history of the sanctuary, gods, and prisoners.

The acts below are narrative gates, not fixed hour counts. Each act should unlock a system because the story makes that system newly relevant.

## Prologue — The Empty Hearth

**Player belief:** the tavern is a failing business at a dangerous crossroads.

**Opening image:** the Keeper cleans the same glass twice because there are no customers. Outside, abandoned wagons and military checkpoints show how the dungeon dispute killed ordinary travel.

**Inciting arrival:** Alden, a young Nordian Warrior, enters as the Keeper is considering closure. He has courage and little else. The Keeper offers a conditional sponsorship: gear, food, one potion, and a debt or share to be paid from recovered loot.

**Tutorial route:**

`Hearth -> Briarway forest -> ancient crypt -> prison threshold -> Balor's domain`

The current project already implements Forest, Stone Crypt, and Balor's Hell as separate playable dungeons. The narrative should eventually treat them as one connected expedition route, but reconnecting runtimes is implementation work and must follow save-flow tests.

**Teaching purpose:** preparation, the Keeper/adventurer relationship, basic combat, extraction, Contribution, and the difference between a worldly approach and an extradimensional prison.

**Branch:** Alden survives or dies. Both outcomes establish the core business, but change resources, memorial state, later dialogue, and Nordian public opinion.

## Act I — The Last Customer

**Primary dramatic question:** can sponsoring adventurers keep the Hearth open?

The Keeper learns that Alden's expedition generated value beyond saleable loot. Gods and common people recognize Contribution. A victory draws attention; a death draws witnesses, warnings, and a bounty for how far Alden reached.

Brina and Eamon arrive next. Brina has a Nordian connection to Alden. Eamon is the first Zarabian Mage and disputes the Nordian claim that every prisoner can simply be killed.

**Systems introduced:** candidate conversation, appraisal, loadout investment, memorial/report, first two-person party, basic reputation.

**Act turn:** the Keeper stops waiting for travelers and deliberately operates an expedition company from the tavern.

## Act II — Opportunity Has Teeth

**Primary dramatic question:** is the dungeon economy saving the region or teaching everyone to profit from instability?

The second authored route is the playable Cinderway:

`Ashen Farmstead -> Sunken Mine -> Ember Foundry`

Adragor's toxic heat leaks into the Cinder Fields, follows the Drowned Veins into the prison conduit, and terminates at an ancient furnace guarded by the Last War-Machine. The route preserves all three existing biome identities rather than pretending they are desert assets. At its keystone, the Keeper can suppress, rebind, recover evidence, or test a new covenant.

**Systems introduced:** dungeon pressure/recovery, competing destinations, scarcity-driven route choice, nation reputation, doctrine-specific clear objectives.

**Act turn:** successful suppression creates demand, and demand brings political ownership claims to the tavern.

## Act III — The Crossroads

**Primary dramatic question:** can people who need one another survive their inherited conflicts?

Helvetica sends the first Healer/cleric and an official observer. Recruitment expands beyond Nordia and Zarabia. Personal compatibility becomes mechanically relevant: ideology, national history, faith, temperament, and prior expedition behavior affect trust.

The remaining three nations enter in staggered waves so each arrival changes the social grammar of the Hearth rather than filling a faction menu at once.

**Systems introduced:** four-person parties, compatibility, national and religious identity, faction requests, neutral-ground rules, Healer and later class access.

**Existing content to use:** curated biographies, candidate conversations, traits, memorials, Hall of Heroes, calendar events, and descendants can carry this act once identities and relationship fields are expanded.

**Act turn:** a party that is tactically strong but politically incompatible creates the first preventable expedition disaster—or succeeds and triggers an international dispute over credit.

## Act IV — The Great Game

**Primary dramatic question:** who benefits from the Keeper's neutrality?

All six nations and multiple gods recognize the Hearth's strategic value. The Keeper receives mutually incompatible contracts, doctrines, boons, embargo threats, and propaganda demands. Suppressed dungeons recover; neglected ones become lucrative and dangerous.

The existing dungeons become territorial story fronts rather than a purely linear difficulty ladder:

| Current dungeon | Recommended story function |
|---|---|
| Verdant Forest / Briarway | Nordian approach to the Balor prison and proof that the land around an entrance mutates |
| Stone Crypt | Mortal ossuary grown around or deliberately hiding Balor's threshold; Necromancer/Crypt Lord is gatekeeper, not final prisoner |
| Balor's Hell | First unmistakable divine prison and the apparent proof of Nordian doctrine |
| Ashen Farmstead | Civilian territory damaged by leakage and war logistics; demonstrates that outbreaks ruin ordinary livelihoods |
| Sunken Mine | Resource rush and labor conflict; nations value ore while something below the waterline wakes |
| Ember Foundry | Evidence that ancient prison technology was weaponized in an earlier war |
| Moonlit Grove | A non-hostile or conditionally hostile power that complicates the gods-versus-prisoners story |
| Abyssal Archive | Records that contradict official creation histories and foreshadow why the gods cannot intervene |

**Systems introduced:** favor per deity, boons with obligations, claims/credit, world events, pressure and outbreak forecasting, richer expedition objectives than “kill boss.”

**Act turn:** the Keeper discovers that the gods are not merely rewarding heroism; they are using the Hearth to reach prisons they can no longer maintain.

## Act V — Fracture

**Primary dramatic question:** can the Hearth prevent political war while keeping the supernatural front contained?

Road closures, border incidents, refugees, faction ultimatums, and synchronized dungeon surges squeeze the tavern. A high-reputation Hearth becomes more valuable and therefore more contested. A low-reputation Hearth loses the cooperation necessary to coordinate suppression.

The Keeper learns that national doctrines each preserve a fragment of the original covenant. No single doctrine can repair the system alone.

**Systems introduced:** crisis calendar, cross-front tradeoffs, sanctuary violations, faction coalitions, first covenant fragments.

**Existing systems to extend:** calendar history can become a world-event ledger; reputation can split into public renown plus per-faction standing; clues can become structured lore/covenant discoveries.

**Act turn:** an apparent solution strengthens one seal while pushing another past outbreak, proving the prison network is interconnected.

## Act VI — Ruin

**Primary dramatic question:** what remains worth doing when this timeline cannot be saved?

The collapse should play rather than appear as a conventional “Game Over.” Reports accelerate. Nations mobilize. Routes vanish. Dungeon enemies appear outside their regions. Refugees and wounded survivors fill the Hearth. Eventually the sanctuary is attacked and destroyed.

Player performance must still matter during collapse. It determines:

- who reaches the Hearth;
- which nations blame or defend the Keeper;
- which truths and relics can be bound to the sanctuary;
- which character scenes occur;
- what the Keeper carries as knowledge into the reset.

**Committed structure:** Loop Zero is inevitably terminal once its crisis clock matures because every ending requires remembered evidence. Excellent play improves the inheritance and reveals that lack of knowledge—not arbitrary difficulty—made the first timeline unwinnable. Later loops can end the crisis through one of four evidence- and relationship-gated resolutions.

**Final image:** fire consumes the Hearth; an older voice, mechanism, or memory says “Again.”

## Act VII — Again

The Keeper wakes on the original morning. Bills, glass, weather, and Alden's entrance repeat. The player now understands that the campaign is a loop.

The first repeated conversation must offer at least one impossible foreknowledge choice. This is the emotional proof of persistence, not merely a new upgrade screen.

**Systems introduced:** Keeper Memory, loop number, remembered dialogue, knowledge-gated interventions, sanctuary-bound unlocks, revised objectives.

**Act turn:** the Keeper deliberately changes Alden's preparation or route and sees history diverge.

## The Remembered Campaign

Later loops should not replay seven identical acts. They compress known setup and branch around interventions.

### Loop objectives

Each loop asks the player to secure some combination of:

- a surviving witness;
- a covenant fragment;
- a true name;
- a reconciled pair of factions;
- proof against an official doctrine;
- a sanctuary upgrade that can survive reset;
- a different resolution for a prison;
- a relationship strong enough to produce an echo of memory in another person.

### Escalating agency

1. **Prediction:** warn people and prepare better.
2. **Intervention:** change arrivals, routes, loadouts, and credit.
3. **Coalition:** bring rival doctrines into the same solution.
4. **Defiance:** refuse or redirect a god's demand.
5. **Revision:** change how a prison is resolved.
6. **Covenant:** replace the failing order with a sustainable agreement.

### Ending families

Final endings should be designed only after the cosmology decision, but the campaign should support at least three philosophical outcomes:

- **Restoration:** repair the original seals and renew a corrected divine covenant.
- **Liberation:** distinguish threats from unjust prisoners and dismantle selected prisons.
- **Succession:** create a mortal/divine network centered on the Hearth that replaces the old monopoly of the gods.

None should be a simple morality button. Each requires relationships, knowledge, and consequences accumulated across loops.

## Story beat contract

Every major beat added to the game should specify:

- trigger and repeat policy;
- point-of-view participants and portraits;
- player belief before and after;
- mechanical system introduced or changed;
- state written on completion;
- alternate text for Alden alive/dead and pre-/post-loop where relevant;
- failure, postponement, and save/load behavior;
- localization key and content owner;
- testable acceptance condition.

This prevents story scenes from becoming detached dialogue that neither reads nor changes campaign state.
