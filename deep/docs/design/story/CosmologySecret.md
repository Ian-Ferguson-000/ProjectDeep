# Writer-Only Cosmology

## The concealed history

The prisons were not created by a morally unified pantheon. They were built after the War of Unmaking by the **First Concord**, a temporary alliance of gods, mortal sealwrights, and powers that later histories classified as prisoners. The Concord contained entities whose continued freedom threatened mortal reality, but the victors also imprisoned witnesses, defectors, and rivals whose testimony endangered the new divine order.

The prison network is one distributed machine. Each seal interprets destruction, binding, memory, and consent differently; no national doctrine contains the whole repair method. Balor is an immediate existential threat. His danger does not prove that every prisoner is guilty or that every divine sentence remains lawful.

## Why the gods cannot intervene directly

To finish the First Concord, the gods accepted the **Covenant of Distance**. A god's full presence near a prison now resonates through the entire network: repairing one seal directly can open several others and can restore the god's ancient wartime authority over mortal territory. Gods can send signs, lend bounded power, and negotiate through mortals, but direct intervention risks synchronized collapse and violates limits rival gods can enforce.

This is why divine boons are bargains rather than rescue. Some gods hide the limitation out of shame, some call it sacred restraint, and some hope the Hearth will produce a mortal loophole.

## The Hearth

The Wayfarer's Sanctuary was the mortal witness-seat of the First Concord. No nation or pantheon could own it because its Keeper authenticated agreements among mutually hostile powers. The modern tavern inherited that sanctuary without understanding it.

When the Hearth is irrecoverably destroyed, its failsafe rewinds the local covenant to the morning of Alden's arrival. Physical history resets. The Keeper's mantle preserves witnessed knowledge because a witness who forgets cannot enforce a covenant. Loop Zero is inevitably lost: the Keeper lacks the evidence needed to choose an ending. Later loops can avert collapse by completing one of the four ending contracts.

## Ending truth

- **Keeper's Covenant:** revise the prison network with the Hearth as mortal guarantor.
- **Mortal Compact:** place stewardship under a multi-national institution independent of divine ownership.
- **Divine Concord:** bind rival gods to enforceable limits demonstrated through fulfilled bargains.
- **Broken Thrones:** dismantle the divine monopoly and open selected prisons under mortal responsibility.

None is the hidden “good” answer. Each preserves some protections, sacrifices some certainty, and changes who must answer for future harm.
