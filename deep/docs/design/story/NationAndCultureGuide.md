# Nation and Culture Guide

The six display names are current project canon; stable IDs must not be renamed because they are serialized. Cultural inspiration is a research direction, never permission to copy a living religion, sacred name, costume, or accent. Every nation contains class variety, dissent, cross-national worship, and people with ordinary motives.

## Nordia

Northern border holds, oath assemblies, hunting roads, and communities accustomed to defending one another without waiting for distant courts. The state wants decisive control of Balor's route. Destroy doctrine is politically dominant, not biologically or culturally universal. Avoid “all Vikings,” berserker caricatures, and courage-as-masculinity. Brina protects; Lysa questions official naming; Dessa follows a foreign civic-healing tradition.

## Zarabia

A network of covenant academies, river markets, spirit offices, and negotiated public obligations. Authority comes from maintaining relationships and knowing the terms under which power answers. Avoid direct lifts from Yoruba names, orisha, ritual objects, or a generalized “African magic nation.” Eamon is a dissident scholar; Ilyra adopts a Dominion amendment doctrine; faith and summoning are not class requirements.

## Helvetica

Civic leagues organized around law, medicine, roads, temples, and accountable public office. Its leaders treat seals as infrastructure and seek jurisdiction through expertise. Avoid a toga monoculture, emotionless bureaucracy, and healer=priest assumptions. Ulric embodies duty; Soren rejects institutional cruelty; Fara learns across traditions.

## Verdant Concord

Interdependent forest, hill, and river communities governed through old land agreements and seasonal councils. The Concord argues that a prison forfeits legitimacy when its boundary poisons living territory. Avoid generic druids, whimsical fae speech, and nature-as-innocence. Rhea is a crisis healer; Corin follows an amendment doctrine; Joren's beast bond is personal rather than national destiny.

## Veiled Isles

Island households, port cities, ancestor obligations, and local spirit jurisdictions linked by navigation and reciprocal hospitality. The Isles preserve accounts of beings other empires renamed. Avoid literal Shinto vocabulary, ninja monoculture, exoticized masks, and purity-as-racial metaphor. Tamsin is an investigator; Wren is secular and practical; Petra favors Zarabian rebinding.

## Sun-Crowned Dominion

Canal cities, funerary archives, astronomical courts, and legal traditions that understand order as a maintained cycle rather than permanent stasis. The Dominion seeks authority to amend ancient sentences. Avoid copied Egyptian gods, indiscriminate Egyptian/Mesopotamian blending, mummy aesthetics as culture, and timeless-desert stereotypes. Nessa is skeptical, Orin studies defensive history, and Rhea follows a foreign hospitality god.

## Naming and content checks

- Use fictional personal and divine names; do not lightly alter a living sacred name.
- Describe material culture from geography, labor, and institutions—not class role.
- Give every national position a cost, evidence, and named dissenter.
- Do not encode national hostility as an automatic relationship penalty.
- Review new inspiration-specific content for conflation, sacred imagery, and colonial framing before release.
