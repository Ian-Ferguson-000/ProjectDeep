# Narrative Vision

## North star

> You are not the hero. You are the person who makes heroes possible.

The player keeps a failing tavern at the meeting point of six rival nations. When ancient divine prisons begin surfacing, the nations fight over their resources and their gods recruit mortals to suppress what lies inside. The Keeper arms, feeds, houses, and dispatches adventurers, gradually turning a dying roadside business into the one neutral institution capable of preventing war and supernatural collapse.

The first apparent campaign is **Loop Zero**. If the world falls and the tavern is destroyed, the building's forgotten failsafe returns the Keeper to the morning the first Nordian adventurer entered. The Keeper retains knowledge rather than ordinary wealth. Each subsequent loop is an attempt to save people more intelligently, reconcile incompatible powers, understand the prisons, and decide whether the gods deserve obedience.

## Player fantasy

The heroes fight. The Keeper makes the consequential decisions:

- whom to trust, recruit, and place together;
- how much scarce equipment, food, medicine, and training to invest;
- which dungeon is urgent, profitable, politically tolerable, or dangerously neglected;
- which nation and god receives credit for a victory;
- which secrets survive the next collapse;
- whether to destroy, repair, release, or transform what the gods imprisoned.

The game should regularly make a mundane tavern decision carry mythic consequences without making the Keeper feel like a conventional chosen-one combatant.

## Narrative pillars

### Small decisions hold up the world

The emotional unit of the story is not a king's speech. It is deciding whether a young mage gets the second healing potion, whether two rivals can share a room, or whether a wounded veteran should be sent out once more.

### Failure produces knowledge

Death and collapse hurt. They do not become meaningless. Expeditions can leave Contribution, bestiary knowledge, relationship knowledge, routes, recipes, divine favor, or warnings. A reset preserves what belongs to the Keeper's mantle, not the tavern's storeroom.

### Neutrality is active and costly

The tavern is a sanctuary, not an absence of politics. Every faction tries to define neutrality in its own favor. Maintaining the crossroads requires boundaries, bargains, and sometimes refusing all available patrons.

### Every institution tells only part of the truth

The gods call the dungeon lords enemies. Kingdoms call the dungeons territory. Merchants call them opportunity. Survivors call them graves. Some prisoners are existential threats; others may be victims, witnesses, or former allies whose history was erased.

### People are more than builds

Nation, class, faith, personality, history, and allegiance are separate axes. Nordia favors Warriors and Rangers, but a Nordian Mage is possible. A Zarabian may serve a foreign god. The mechanically strongest party may be socially unstable.

## Tone and dramatic curve

The surface begins warm, practical, and faintly funny: unpaid bills, thin stew, boastful recruits, and a Keeper stretching inadequate supplies. The deeper story becomes mournful, uncanny, and politically tense. Hope remains grounded in hospitality and remembered people rather than uncomplicated divine rescue.

The intended curve is:

`failing business -> risky opportunity -> growing company -> neutral crossroads -> political instrument -> failing world -> revealed sanctuary -> informed resistance`

## World foundation

### The Hearth

**Committed direction:** the tavern stands at a crossroads older than the surrounding borders. It was once the Wayfarer's Sanctuary, established when the prisons were made so no kingdom or pantheon would control access to all seals. Roads and settlements grew around it; its sacred function faded into custom and then folklore.

The Keeper is a mantle accepted by taking responsibility for the Hearth. The mantle binds its bearer to the failsafe. The building resets time only when the sanctuary is irrecoverably destroyed, not whenever an adventurer dies.

**Recommended:** keep “the Hearth” as the institution's common name and use “Wayfarer's Sanctuary” for the ancient identity. A final proper tavern name can be chosen later.

### Dungeons

Dungeons are extradimensional prisons whose entrances force themselves into the mortal world as their seals weaken. The hostile forest, crypt, sinkhole, mine, or ruin surrounding an entrance is not necessarily the prison itself; it is a wound, defensive growth, occupied approach, or mortal structure built around it.

Each dungeon tracks four narrative truths:

- **Prisoner:** what is actually contained.
- **Official doctrine:** what each nation or pantheon claims is contained.
- **Leak:** how the failing seal changes the surrounding territory.
- **Suppression consequence:** what temporarily improves—and what becomes worse—when adventurers clear it.

Clearing suppresses a dungeon rather than permanently deleting it. Suppression lowers immediate threat and bounty while the prison recovers. Neglect increases resources and danger until an outbreak becomes possible.

### The gods

The gods cannot simply repair the prisons. The reason is a long-term mystery. Their boons are useful, but also make mortals perform work the gods cannot or will not do.

The story must avoid the simple equation `god = good` and the equally simple reversal `god = secretly evil`. Gods have divided interests, incomplete knowledge, ancient compromises, and reasons to fear particular prisoners. Individual gods should cross class and national boundaries instead of functioning as faction skill trees.

### Contribution and Favor

**Contribution** is the worldly and metaphysical value created by an expedition: mapped routes, recovered knowledge, witnessed sacrifice, faction standing, and proof that a seal can be reached. It explains why a failed run can still advance the Keeper.

**Favor** is a relationship with a god, not a generic currency. Gods reward behaviors that reflect their domains and may object to rival bargains. Favor and nationality must remain independent.

## Six-nation framework

Six nations are the baseline. Only the first three names are inherited from the requested concept; the final three are working names. All cultural inspiration should be transformed into a fictional setting through focused research and sensitivity review rather than directly copying living religions or flattening broad regions into class stereotypes.

| Nation | Status | Affinities, not restrictions | Inspiration direction | Relationship to the divine | Initial dungeon position |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nordia | Current canon | Warrior, Ranger | Norse/Germanic and northern frontier traditions | Gods must be impressed through courage, oaths, sacrifice, and protection | Destroy the prisoners before they break free |
| Zarabia | Current canon | Mage, Summoner | West African civic, scholarly, and relational traditions, transformed through dedicated research | Divine and spiritual powers are approached through relationship, offering, office, and negotiated obligation | Rebinding is wiser than pretending ancient powers can be killed |
| Helvetica | Current canon | Healer, Paladin/Tank | Greco-Roman civic and medical traditions | Gods are served through public duty, law, temple, and institution | The seals are divine law and must be restored correctly |
| Verdant Concord | Current canon | Ranger, Druid/Summoner | Irish/Celtic landscape and covenant traditions | Sacred places and old agreements must be respected | The prisons themselves are poisoning the living world |
| Veiled Isles | Current canon | Rogue, Phantom | Japanese island, household-spirit, and ancestor traditions, non-literally adapted | Spirits permeate ordinary life; obligation and ancestry shape practice | Some prisoners were misnamed or unjustly confined |
| Sun-Crowned Dominion | Current canon | Mage, Summoner, Healer | Egyptian civic, legal, funerary, and astronomical directions without indiscriminate regional blending | Divine order contains chaos through names, cycles, death rites, and preservation | Every breach threatens cosmic order, but the original sentence may be altered lawfully |

Implementation guidance, internal dissent, and forbidden clichés are defined in `NationAndCultureGuide.md`. Authored arrivals and the curated roster supply multiple people who complicate each national stereotype.

## Core cast adaptation

| Character | Current game role | Target role |
|---|---|---|
| The Keeper | Implied company/player after the tutorial | Persistent player viewpoint and holder of the Hearth mantle from the opening onward |
| Alden | Tutorial Warrior sent into the Briarway | Young Nordian and the Last Customer; first sponsored expedition; can survive or die without changing player identity |
| Brina | Founding Warrior inspired by Alden | Nordian veteran or old friend who validates Alden's story and joins the second wave |
| Eamon | Founding Mage from a monastery | First Zarabian Mage; exposes a different doctrine of dungeons and magic |
| Mara Vell | Legacy bartender/owner | Removed as a separate current-canon character; the old asset filename remains for compatibility only |
| Mayor Corvin Rook | Transfers ownership and mediates Mara's claim | Local civic witness and early pressure source; useful for showing road closures, debt, refugees, and common opinion |

**Recommended tutorial convergence:** Alden's victory settles enough debt and proves the model; Alden's death leaves Contribution, intelligence, and possibly a divine bounty. In both branches, the Keeper remains at the Hearth and chooses to sponsor more adventurers. The branch changes Alden's later presence, the memorial, resources, dialogue, and knowledge—not who the player is.

## Mystery ladder

Revelations should answer one question while making the next unavoidable.

1. Why are the roads dying, and can one expedition save the tavern?
2. Why do cleared dungeons recover?
3. Why do six pantheons describe the same prisons differently?
4. Why is the Hearth recognized as neutral by powers older than the nations?
5. Why does the Hearth reset time when destroyed?
6. What did the gods imprison, and which histories were rewritten?
7. Why can the gods no longer maintain the seals themselves?
8. Does saving the world require restoring the old order, freeing parts of it, or making a new covenant?

## Persistence rules

The physical campaign resets: gold, ordinary supplies, inventory, facilities, current recruits, faction borders, dungeon pressure, and most mortal relationships.

The Keeper can retain or unlock: dungeon/bestiary knowledge, recipes and blueprints, remembered dialogue, true names, prophecies, discovered relationship facts, certain divine marks, and a limited number of sanctuary-bound relics.

Remembered knowledge should change choices and dialogue rather than only grant passive bonuses. Characters may sense repetition, but they do not automatically remember prior loops.

## Content principles

- Never make a nation equivalent to a class, biome, skin tone, or single personality.
- Never use a living deity as a lightly renamed vending machine for buffs.
- Give every doctrine evidence, costs, dissenters, and at least one thing it understands better than its rivals.
- Let dungeon lords differ morally. Balor can be an unambiguous immediate threat without proving every divine account true.
- Make death specific through names, relationships, memorial text, and consequences; do not turn casualties into anonymous fuel.
- Expose lore through disputes, expedition reports, objects, and changed conditions before encyclopedic exposition.
- Keep the Keeper's work visible. Credit, blame, bills, hospitality, and preparation should appear in major scenes.

## Canon decisions

1. The Keeper is an authored but unnamed role.
2. Mara is removed as a separate owner/antagonist; her name survives only in a legacy portrait path.
3. Loop Zero is inevitably terminal because ending evidence requires at least one remembered loop. Later loops can avert collapse.
4. Alden becomes a Nordian public symbol after victory or a named memorial after death; he never replaces the Keeper.
5. Adragor's route is the existing Cinderway: Ashen Farmstead, Sunken Mine, and Ember Foundry, with their existing identities preserved rather than cosmetically renamed as desert content.
6. Active scope is Warrior, Mage, Healer, Tank, Rogue, and Summoner. Ranger, Druid, Paladin, and Phantom are not promised campaign classes.
7. Refusing patronage is viable. Mortal improvements, evidence, Contribution, compatibility, and faction coalitions remain available without divine boons.
8. The First Concord, Covenant of Distance, mixed prisoner history, and divine limitation are fixed in `CosmologySecret.md`.
9. Final victory is one of four prerequisite-driven, authored ending contracts. Choosing one halts further crisis escalation and does not reset.
10. “Eros” is not used as the implemented narrative title; the product title remains a separate publishing decision.
